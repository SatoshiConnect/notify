# Mobile interaction decisions

This document records why the mobile layout is organized this way. It does not claim the interface has passed user research. That requires observing people use it on their own phones.

## Decisions in the current iteration

| Decision | Evidence | What the interface does | How to verify it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Use four persistent, labeled communication destinations on phones: Contacts, Calls, Chats, and Settings. | The user's supplied LINE screenshots show labeled bottom navigation and distinct settings; Apple HIG recommends stable, labeled top-level navigation. Four is a product choice from the supplied reference, not a research finding. | The bottom bar keeps all four app routes available. Desktop uses a compact icon rail plus chat categories. | Test task completion and wrong turns with iPhone users; reduce or regroup destinations if they miss tabs or cannot predict them. |
| Follow the supplied LINE screen hierarchy while keeping NSAT's own identity and data model. | The user's screenshots are the product reference. WCAG 2.2 requires at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text; Apple advises checking contrast and not relying on color alone. | The signed-in shell uses a slim dark slate rail, a labeled category strip, a searchable conversation list, and a separate conversation pane; mobile uses safe-area-aware bottom navigation. Neutral surfaces and a restrained green accent distinguish selection. | Measure final text and control contrast, then test the chat, Contacts, Calls, and Settings tasks on an iPhone and a smaller phone. |
| Keep core communication actions easy to reach and reduce controls in the first view. | The 2018 cross-platform messenger evaluation reports information overload, unclear organization, OS inconsistency, and clipped content; its recommendations say main features should be easy to access and adapt to the target OS. | The first view of People contains requests, friends, and Add friends. Account, room-management, and search tools are moved under “More.” Calls opens to call-ready people instead of blank video frames and effect controls. | Observe users finding a friend, opening a chat, and starting a call. Note errors, task time, and where users hesitate. |
| Offer voice and video as explicit call choices. | LINE documents separate “Voice call” and “Video call” choices from a chat or friend entry, including on mobile. | The Calls view offers Voice and Video buttons for each person in the current chat; voice calls request microphone access only and hide video-only controls. | Ask users to place both call types and confirm they can predict which permissions each will request. |
| Keep direct chats and rooms in one searchable list, with category filters. | Caro-Alvaro et al. (2018) reported overload and confusion around organization; their recommendations call for making core features easy to find and visually differentiating individual and group conversations. Caro-Alvaro et al. (2024) found that visually separated direct/group conversations significantly facilitated task performance. | The desktop layout puts the filter strip above a single list; direct and shared-room rows carry distinct icons and subtitles. Filters use only categories supported by this backend. | Ask users to find a named direct chat and a named shared room; record wrong selections and time. |
| Put a call entry point in the conversation header and keep Calls in the bottom navigation. | LINE's current help for Thailand documents starting a call from the top-right of a chat or from the Friends list. Apple's guidance also calls for stable, labeled navigation. | “Call” in a conversation starts a call when one other participant is online, otherwise opens the Calls tab; Calls stays visible as a top-level destination. | Ask users to call the person in the open conversation and then find the Calls section from another screen. |
| Keep friend discovery visible at the top of People and use a known identifier. | The 2024 study reports significant performance differences in adding a contact and recommends adding by ID. LINE's Thai help describes a Friends entry within Chats. | “Add friends” is the main action on People and searches by username or exact Gmail. | Ask users to find and add a known username without assistance. |
| Preserve the device status area and safe-area spacing. | The 2018 evaluation found that some messenger overlays hid the system status bar and recommended keeping it visible; it also recommends adapting to OS layout conventions. | The app accounts for the iPhone top and bottom safe areas and keeps its connection state in a compact app header. | Check on an iPhone 14 Pro Max with the keyboard open and in portrait and landscape orientations. |

## Research and platform references

- Caro-Alvaro et al. (2024), [Measuring the Effects of Usability Recommendations for Mobile Instant Messaging Apps on User Performance and User Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2024.2303553), *International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction*. The experiment compared three established messaging apps with an app built from messaging-specific recommendations; the authors report significantly better task performance for the recommendation-based app. They also found that close contacts on a service predict intent to continue using it more strongly than feature count or interface acceptance.
- Caro-Alvaro et al. (2018), [Identifying Usability Issues in Instant Messaging Apps on iOS and Android Platforms](https://doi.org/10.1155/2018/2056942), *Mobile Information Systems*. The authors reported issues with task completion, visual cohesion, information overload, OS mismatch, hidden status areas, and clipped layouts. Their recommendations include making core features easy to access, keeping the status bar visible, adapting to the OS, and visually distinguishing individual and group chats.
- Apple, [Human Interface Guidelines: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars/). The guidance describes tab bars as top-level navigation, recommends visible labels and stable availability, and warns against overflow.
- W3C, [WCAG 2.2: Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). Normal text needs 4.5:1 contrast; large text needs 3:1.
- Apple, [Human Interface Guidelines: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color). The guidance recommends sufficient contrast, avoiding color as the only cue, and adapting colors for appearance and contrast settings.
- LINE Help Center, [Making voice/video calls](https://help.line.me/line/smartphone?contentId=20000270&ctype=article&lang=en). For Thailand, LINE documents calling from the Friends area and from the call icon in a chat.
- LINE Help Center, [About the redesign of the LINE app](https://help.line.me/line/smartphone/?contentId=200001630&lang=en). The Thailand rollout adds an OpenChat bottom tab and a Friends tab within Chats; rollout may vary by account and app version.

## Limits and evaluation gate

The research informs design choices but does not establish that this specific interface suits NSAT Messenger users. Before calling the mobile redesign successful, test on an iPhone 14 Pro Max and at least one smaller phone. Have users complete these tasks without coaching: open a direct chat, identify a shared room, add a friend, accept a request, and start/end a video call. Record completion, time, wrong turns, and user comments. Fix any task that users cannot complete reliably before adding more mobile controls.
